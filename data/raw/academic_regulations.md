# Demo University — Academic & Student Regulations 2026

**Fictional corpus for RuleLens demonstration and evaluation. Not a real university rulebook.**

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Authority

These Academic and Student Regulations establish the baseline rules for undergraduate academic administration, student responsibilities, assessment, progression, fees, scholarships, residential life, facilities, conduct, appeals, and graduation at Demo University. The regulations are written as a self-contained fictional corpus for the RuleLens demonstration system. They are not the regulations of a real university and must not be presented as such.

The regulations apply to students enrolled in undergraduate programs during the 2026 academic year unless a provision expressly identifies a different population, program, term, or administrative process. Where a program handbook contains additional requirements, those requirements apply only where these regulations authorize program-specific procedures or where an authorized academic body has issued a clearly identified exception.

The Academic Council is the University's principal academic policy-making body for the purposes of this corpus. The Academic Office administers general academic processes, including registration and academic records. The Finance Office administers fees and financial accounts. The Financial Aid Office administers scholarships and financial assistance. The Student Affairs Office coordinates student services, residential matters, and general student support. The Examination Office coordinates examinations and examination records.

A rule in this corpus should be interpreted according to its ordinary wording, stated scope, and stated conditions. A heading, table, footnote, exception, or notice should not be ignored when determining whether a provision applies. Where two provisions appear to impose incompatible requirements on the same situation, RuleLens should expose the evidence rather than silently selecting one provision.

The absence of a rule is not permission. If a student asks a question that this corpus does not answer, the correct response is that the available regulations do not contain sufficient information. Students should then be directed to the office or authority most closely connected to the subject of the question.

## 2. Academic Calendar and Semesters

The academic year contains two regular semesters, identified as Semester I and Semester II, together with an optional summer term. Semester I normally begins in August and Semester II normally begins in January. Exact teaching, examination, holiday, and registration dates are published by the Academic Office for each academic cycle.

The academic calendar is the controlling source for dates when a process depends on a calendar deadline. A student is responsible for reviewing the published calendar and notices relevant to the student's program. A general statement in these regulations does not replace a specific date published in an official notice unless the notice expressly states that it is only illustrative.

The summer term may be offered for selected courses, reassessment opportunities, or other academic purposes. Availability depends on staffing, enrollment, course requirements, and approval by the relevant academic authority. The existence of a summer term does not mean that every course is offered during that term.

Students who are required to complete a particular course before a later course must satisfy that prerequisite before registering for the later course. A prerequisite may be identified in the program curriculum or course catalog. These regulations do not establish a universal list of prerequisites.

Changes to the academic calendar may be announced by the Academic Office when circumstances require adjustment. A revised notice should identify the affected dates and processes. Students should rely on the latest applicable official notice when a later notice clearly supersedes an earlier calendar entry.

A calendar date and a procedural deadline are not necessarily the same thing. For example, a semester may begin on a particular date while course registration closes earlier. Students should therefore distinguish between the start of teaching, registration deadlines, fee deadlines, withdrawal deadlines, and examination dates.

## 3. Admission, Enrollment, and Student Status

A student becomes enrolled after completing the University's admission and enrollment requirements. Enrollment may require submission of academic records, identity documents, photographs, declarations, and other materials identified in the admission notice. This corpus does not establish an exhaustive list of admission documents.

A student's academic record is maintained by the Academic Office. Students should promptly report apparent errors in names, course registrations, grades, or other academic records through the prescribed administrative process. An informal conversation with an instructor does not by itself amend an official academic record.

Student status may be active, on approved leave, suspended, withdrawn, or another status established by the Academic Office. The consequences of each status depend on the applicable rules. A student should not assume that an administrative request has changed status until the relevant authority has recorded or communicated the decision.

Enrollment in a program does not automatically establish eligibility for every course, scholarship, facility, or examination. Separate eligibility conditions may apply. Where an application requires academic or financial clearance, the relevant clearance must be completed before the process is considered final.

Students must use their assigned student identification information for official University systems. Sharing credentials may expose a student to unauthorized access and may violate information-security or conduct rules. The regulations do not establish a specific technical penalty for every credential-related incident; such incidents may instead be handled under applicable conduct or security procedures.

A student who intends to withdraw from the University should follow the withdrawal process specified by the Academic Office. The date on which withdrawal becomes effective may affect fees, academic records, housing, and other services. This corpus does not prescribe a universal refund formula for withdrawal.

Students remain responsible for obligations incurred before a change in student status. For example, a student who leaves a residence may still need to return University property and clear outstanding charges.

## 4. Course Registration and Add/Drop

Students must complete course registration within the registration window published by the Academic Office. Registration is confirmed only after required academic and financial checks are complete. A student should review the final registered-course list and report discrepancies promptly.

A student who misses the ordinary registration window may submit a late-registration request within five calendar days after the registration deadline. Late registration is subject to approval by the Academic Office and payment of the applicable late-registration fee. Submission of a request does not guarantee approval.

A registration hold may be placed when a student has an unresolved financial, library, disciplinary, or administrative obligation. A student with a registration hold may not add new courses until the hold is cleared. The fact that a hold exists does not necessarily cancel courses that were already validly registered; the effect of a particular hold should be confirmed by the responsible office.

Course additions and withdrawals are subject to the deadlines and procedures published for the academic term. These regulations do not establish a universal withdrawal date for every course. A student should therefore not infer a specific withdrawal deadline from the existence of the registration period.

Where a course has a prerequisite, students must satisfy the prerequisite before registering unless an authorized waiver has been issued. An instructor's informal permission is not necessarily an official prerequisite waiver.

Students should consider workload, timetable conflicts, assessment schedules, and progression requirements before adding a course. Academic advising may help students evaluate these issues, but the student remains responsible for ensuring that the final registration is valid.

A course may be cancelled, rescheduled, or otherwise modified when enrollment or operational circumstances require it. The University should communicate material changes through the responsible academic office. The corpus does not guarantee that every registered course will be offered in an unchanged format.

A student who registers for a course but fails to meet a mandatory requirement may be prevented from completing the course or assessment. The consequences depend on the course rules and applicable academic procedures.

## 5. Attendance and Participation

Students are expected to attend scheduled teaching activities. A student must maintain at least 75% attendance in a course to be eligible for the regular end-semester examination under the general attendance rule in this corpus.

Attendance is normally calculated from scheduled teaching activities recorded by the instructor or department. Different types of activities may have separate attendance records when the course design requires them. This corpus does not establish a universal formula for converting attendance between lectures, tutorials, laboratories, workshops, or field activities.

A student who is absent for documented medical reasons may submit supporting documentation to the department within seven calendar days of returning to classes. Approved medical absence may be recorded separately from ordinary absence. Approval is not automatic, and submission of a medical document does not by itself establish examination eligibility.

A student below the ordinary attendance threshold may submit an attendance-condonation request when permitted by the department. Approval is discretionary and does not automatically restore examination eligibility. The existence of a condonation process should not be interpreted as a guarantee that every shortage will be condoned.

For laboratory courses, attendance is calculated using scheduled laboratory sessions. Missing a laboratory session may also require completion of a make-up activity where the instructor permits one. A make-up activity does not necessarily erase the original absence from the attendance record.

Students should notify instructors or the relevant office when an absence is foreseeable. Notification is useful for administration but does not automatically convert an absence into approved leave or medical absence.

Attendance records should be reviewed by students during the period in which corrections can reasonably be made. If a student believes an attendance entry is incorrect, the student should raise the issue with the instructor or department and provide relevant evidence. These regulations do not specify a universal number of days for every attendance correction request.

The 75% requirement stated in Section 5.1 applies to regular end-semester examination eligibility. A special examination may have additional conditions communicated in its approval notice. Students should therefore distinguish between regular examination eligibility and special examination eligibility.

## 6. Examinations and Assessment

A student who satisfies course attendance requirements and has completed required academic work may appear in the regular end-semester examination. Course-specific assessment requirements may include assignments, laboratories, projects, presentations, or other components.

A student who misses an examination must submit an absence explanation to the Academic Office within three working days. Approval of a special examination is not automatic. The explanation should identify the examination, the date, and the circumstances of the absence, together with supporting evidence where relevant.

Where the Academic Office approves a special examination, the student must follow the schedule and conditions communicated in the approval notice. The notice may contain additional conditions. This corpus does not state that every approved special examination follows the same timetable.

Assessment may include continuous assessment and end-semester assessment. The precise weighting of individual components belongs to the course information or program documentation and is not established universally by this corpus.

Examination misconduct includes cheating, impersonation, unauthorized collaboration, possession of prohibited materials, or other examination misconduct. Allegations may result in disciplinary proceedings and penalties under the Student Conduct Code. The regulations do not prescribe a single automatic penalty for every allegation.

Students must follow instructions issued by examination supervisors. A student who does not understand an examination instruction should seek clarification through the permitted examination procedure rather than relying on assumptions.

A student's examination record is maintained by the Examination Office. Corrections to recorded marks or grades should follow the official review process. A student should preserve relevant evidence and submit requests within applicable deadlines.

Where an examination requires a calculator, reference sheet, identification document, or other item, the course or examination instructions should specify the permitted materials. These general regulations do not establish a universal list of permitted devices for every examination.

The University may use different assessment arrangements for practical, laboratory, project, or performance-based courses. The existence of a general examination rule does not imply that every course uses an identical assessment format.

## 7. Grading, Grade Review, and Reassessment

The University uses letter grades from A through F. A grade of F is a failing grade. The detailed conversion between numerical marks and letter grades is not specified in this corpus and should not be invented by the system.

A student may request a review of a recorded grade within ten calendar days of official publication of the grade. The request must identify the course and the specific basis for review. A request should be submitted through the designated academic process.

A grade review is not an automatic grade increase. The responsible authority reviews the request according to the applicable assessment records and procedures. The outcome may confirm the existing grade or result in another authorized change.

A student who fails a course may be eligible for a reassessment opportunity if the course and academic calendar provide one. Reassessment does not guarantee a passing result. The format, date, eligibility requirements, and grade treatment may depend on the relevant course or examination notice.

Where an improvement examination is offered, the improved grade is recorded according to the rules published for that examination cycle. This corpus does not specify a universal maximum number of improvement attempts.

Students should distinguish between grade review, reassessment, and grade improvement. A grade review examines a recorded result; reassessment provides an additional assessment opportunity where offered; improvement concerns an authorized opportunity to seek an improved result. The precise rules for each process must be taken from the applicable notice.

A failing grade may create an academic backlog when the course is required for the program or when the Academic Office classifies the unresolved requirement as a backlog. Not every academic concern is necessarily a backlog.

Students should not assume that passing one assessment component automatically passes the course. Course-level requirements and assessment structures determine the final result.

## 8. Academic Progression and Backlogs

Academic standing is determined at the end of each regular semester using the student's completed credits, grades, and applicable progression rules. The progression rules for a specific program may contain additional conditions.

A backlog is created when a student receives a failing grade in a course that is required for the student's program or when the student otherwise has an unresolved academic requirement classified as a backlog by the Academic Office.

Students with unresolved academic requirements may be reviewed for progression. The review considers the program's published progression requirements and any approved exceptions. A student should not infer a universal progression threshold from the presence of a backlog alone.

When repeat enrollment is required, the student must register for the course in the next available offering unless the Academic Office approves a different arrangement. If a course is not offered in the expected term, the student should seek guidance from the Academic Office.

Progression decisions should be communicated through the appropriate academic process. A student who disagrees with a decision may use the applicable appeal process where one exists.

Academic progression and placement eligibility are separate concepts unless an official rule expressly connects them. These regulations do not establish a general minimum CGPA for placement registration.

A student's total credits, failed courses, current enrollment, and program requirements may all affect progression. Because program structures differ, a question about whether a specific student can progress cannot always be answered from these general regulations alone.

Students nearing graduation should review outstanding requirements early. The University may identify academic or administrative holds that need to be cleared before degree processing.

## 9. Scholarships and Financial Assistance

University-funded scholarships are subject to the eligibility criteria stated in the relevant scholarship notice. A scholarship is not automatically awarded merely because a student meets one eligibility criterion.

For the Demo University Merit Scholarship, an undergraduate student must have a cumulative GPA of at least 8.00/10.00 and must have no active disciplinary penalty at the time of award. This provision is the general-regulations statement for the scholarship and is intentionally paired with a separate scholarship notice in the RuleLens demonstration corpus.

Scholarship applications must be submitted through the designated portal by the deadline stated in the scholarship notice. Applications submitted after the deadline are ordinarily not considered. The corpus does not establish a universal late-application grace period.

The Financial Aid Office may request documents necessary to verify eligibility. Failure to provide requested documentation may delay or prevent an award. Students are responsible for providing accurate information.

A scholarship may be subject to renewal conditions in a separate notice. These regulations do not establish a universal renewal GPA, income threshold, or semester-by-semester retention condition for every scholarship.

The absence of a general scholarship condition should not be interpreted as evidence that no condition exists. If the question concerns a particular scholarship, the relevant scholarship notice should be consulted.

Scholarship decisions may be subject to administrative review where an applicable process provides one. The existence of an appeal or review process does not guarantee a change in the award decision.

Students receiving financial assistance should keep copies of applications, receipts, and submitted documents. The University may use official records to verify eligibility and disbursement.

## 10. Fees, Payment, Refunds, and Financial Holds

University fees may be paid through the University's designated online payment portal or another payment method officially announced by the Finance Office. Students should retain payment receipts and transaction references.

The standard academic fee schedule is reproduced in a separate mixed-format fee notice and in the fee table included in this corpus. The semester tuition deadline in the main regulations is 15 August 2026. The separate fee notice intentionally states 20 August 2026 for the same fee item, creating a contradiction for RuleLens evaluation.

A student who does not pay a required fee by its applicable deadline may incur a late fee or another administrative restriction stated in the fee notice. The exact monetary amount of a late fee is not established by these regulations.

A student who believes a fee has been charged incorrectly may submit a written dispute to the Finance Office within ten calendar days of the charge appearing in the student account. The dispute should identify the charge and explain the basis for the objection.

Financial obligations may result in an administrative or registration hold. A hold may prevent course additions or other processes until the underlying issue is resolved. The specific consequences of a particular hold may depend on the responsible office.

Refunds, where available, are governed by the applicable fee or withdrawal notice. These regulations do not establish a single refund percentage for every withdrawal date.

Students should not assume that paying a fee late automatically preserves every academic or administrative right. A fee notice may identify a specific consequence, such as blocked examination registration or withheld room allocation.

If a payment appears to have failed but money has been deducted from a bank account, the student should preserve the transaction evidence and contact the Finance Office or designated payment support channel. The corpus does not prescribe a universal processing time for payment reversals.

## 11. Hostel and Residential Life

Hostel allocation is subject to availability, program requirements, and the University's residential rules. An allocation is not guaranteed merely because a student submits an application.

Students receiving a room allocation must complete the required residential check-in process. Check-in may require identity verification, payment clearance, acceptance of residential rules, and return of required forms.

Students residing in University housing must follow the Residential Conduct Rules, including quiet hours, visitor restrictions, safety requirements, and instructions from residential staff. This corpus does not establish the exact clock time for quiet hours.

Students are responsible for reasonable care of assigned rooms, furniture, keys, access cards, and other University property. Loss or damage may result in a charge or administrative action under applicable residential procedures.

Visitors may be subject to registration or other restrictions. The precise visitor hours and registration method are not established by this corpus.

Residential allocation may be withdrawn or changed for administrative, disciplinary, safety, or operational reasons where authorized by the applicable residential rules. A student should not assume that a room assignment is permanent.

The hostel fee is listed in the fee schedule with a standard deadline of 10 August 2026. The listed consequence of missing that deadline is that room allocation may be withheld.

Students leaving University housing should complete the checkout process and return University property. Outstanding charges or unreturned property may delay financial or administrative clearance.

The University may publish additional residential notices. Where a residential notice provides a specific procedure under authority granted by these regulations, the notice should be considered together with the general provisions.

## 12. Library and Campus Facilities

Students may borrow library materials subject to the borrowing limits applicable to their student category. Overdue materials may result in fines or temporary borrowing restrictions.

The library renewal fee appears in the fee schedule with a standard deadline of 30 August 2026. The stated consequence is that library renewal may be restricted. The exact amount of the fee or overdue fine is not established in this corpus.

Students are expected to return borrowed materials when due. If an item is lost or damaged, the student should follow the library's replacement or payment procedure. This corpus does not establish the replacement price for individual materials.

Campus facilities may include laboratories, study rooms, sports spaces, computing facilities, and other services. Access conditions can vary by facility. These regulations do not guarantee access to every facility at all times.

Students must follow safety instructions in laboratories and specialized spaces. A facility supervisor may restrict access when a student does not meet required safety conditions.

Use of University computing resources is subject to applicable security and acceptable-use requirements. Students should not attempt to bypass access controls or interfere with another user's account.

The University may temporarily close or restrict a facility for maintenance, examinations, safety, or other operational reasons. The corpus does not establish a universal notice period for every closure.

Questions about a specific library fine, equipment loan, study-room booking, or facility timetable should be answered only if the relevant provision is present in the available corpus.

## 13. Student Leave and Medical Documentation

Students who need to be absent from classes for personal reasons should inform the relevant academic office or instructor according to departmental procedure. Informing an instructor does not automatically create an approved leave record.

Extended leave requires formal approval. The approval may specify conditions concerning coursework, attendance, examinations, or return to study.

Approval of leave does not by itself guarantee permission to miss an examination. Examination absence is governed by the examination provisions in these regulations.

A student absent for documented medical reasons may submit supporting documentation to the department within seven calendar days of returning to classes. The department may assess the documentation under the applicable procedure.

Medical documentation should be genuine, legible, and relevant to the period of absence. This corpus does not specify a particular doctor, hospital, certificate format, or diagnosis that must be used.

Approved medical absence may be recorded separately from ordinary absence. This does not mean that every medical absence automatically waives an attendance requirement.

Students who need extended medical leave should contact the appropriate academic office promptly because academic consequences may depend on the length and timing of the absence.

Leave arrangements may include conditions for returning to classes, completing missed work, or participating in assessments. The exact arrangement should be taken from the approval communication.

A student should not treat silence from an office as approval of leave. Unless an applicable process says otherwise, approval should be confirmed through the designated authority.

## 14. Student Conduct and Discipline

Students must comply with University rules and must not engage in conduct that materially disrupts teaching, assessment, residential life, or University operations.

Alleged misconduct may be referred to the appropriate disciplinary authority. The student may be asked to provide an explanation or participate in a disciplinary process. An allegation is not itself a final finding.

Depending on the finding and applicable rules, penalties may include warning, monetary penalty, restriction of privileges, suspension, or other measures authorized by the Student Conduct Code.

Examination misconduct includes cheating, impersonation, unauthorized collaboration, possession of prohibited materials, or other prohibited conduct. Examination incidents may be handled through disciplinary procedures and may also affect examination results where authorized.

Students should comply with reasonable instructions from University staff acting within their authority. A disagreement with an instruction does not automatically make non-compliance permissible.

Damage to University property may be investigated. Where responsibility is established, the University may seek recovery of an applicable cost or impose another authorized measure.

Students may be asked to attend a disciplinary meeting or provide a written statement. The timing and format of such a process may be communicated by the responsible authority.

The corpus does not provide a universal penalty schedule for every type of misconduct. RuleLens must not invent a fixed suspension length, fine amount, or other sanction unless that detail is explicitly present in the evidence.

A student subject to a disciplinary decision may use an appeal process where an applicable rule provides one. The general appeal timing provision may apply when no more specific deadline is stated.

## 15. Graduation and Degree Completion

A student may graduate only after completing the credit, course, assessment, and other requirements prescribed for the student's program.

All required courses must be successfully completed unless an approved program-specific exception applies. A student should review the official academic record before the final semester to identify unresolved requirements.

Graduation processing may require financial clearance and return of University property. Outstanding financial obligations, library materials, hostel property, or other administrative matters may need to be resolved before completion of the process.

The final academic record is prepared by the Academic Office using authorized records. Students should review records for apparent errors before the degree is issued.

The University may conduct a graduation verification process. A student who is academically eligible but has an unresolved administrative requirement may be required to clear that requirement before final processing.

These regulations do not establish a universal maximum degree duration. If a program has a maximum completion period, the applicable program rules should be consulted.

These regulations do not establish a universal minimum CGPA for graduation. A student should not infer a graduation CGPA threshold from the merit scholarship requirement.

A student with an unresolved backlog is ordinarily not complete for graduation purposes if the backlog represents a required course or academic requirement. Program-specific exceptions must be supported by an authorized provision.

Students should follow published instructions concerning degree applications, convocation, certificates, transcripts, and collection of academic documents. The exact date and fee for each process may be contained in a separate notice.

## 16. Appeals and Administrative Review

A student who wishes to appeal an academic or administrative decision should use the appeal process specified by the office responsible for that decision.

Unless a more specific deadline is stated in the applicable rule, an appeal should be submitted within fifteen calendar days of the decision. Where a specific rule states a different deadline, the specific deadline governs that appeal.

An appeal should identify the decision being challenged and the basis for the appeal. Supporting documents may be attached where relevant. The existence of an appeal process does not guarantee that the decision will be changed.

Where a matter is expressly assigned to a designated committee or authority, that body makes the decision according to the applicable regulations.

Students should distinguish between asking for information, requesting a correction, seeking a review, and filing an appeal. The appropriate process depends on the nature of the issue.

An office may reject an appeal that is submitted outside the applicable deadline or through an unauthorized channel when the governing procedure requires compliance with those conditions. This corpus does not establish whether late appeals can ever be accepted for exceptional reasons.

An appeal should not be used to invent a rule that is absent from the regulations. If the underlying issue concerns a matter not addressed by this corpus, the student should seek clarification from the responsible authority.

Where two published provisions appear to impose incompatible requirements on the same situation, the conflict should be referred to the designated academic authority rather than resolved by assumption.

## 17. Interpretation, Exceptions, and Silence

The regulations provide baseline academic and student rules for the 2026 academic year. Their provisions should be read according to their stated scope and conditions.

The 75% attendance requirement applies to regular end-semester examination eligibility under the general attendance rule. A special examination may have additional conditions communicated in its approval notice.

The merit scholarship GPA threshold in Section 9.2 applies specifically to the Demo University Merit Scholarship. It does not establish a general minimum GPA for all scholarships.

A department may publish additional procedural requirements where these regulations expressly permit departmental procedures. A departmental procedure must not silently override a University-wide rule unless an authorized exception is stated.

If a question is not addressed by these regulations, the absence of a rule in this corpus must not be interpreted as permission or prohibition.

If two provisions in the University's published regulations appear to impose incompatible requirements on the same situation, the conflict should be referred to the designated academic authority rather than resolved by assumption.

An exception should be treated as an exception only when its scope, condition, or authority is identifiable. A rule that applies to one scholarship should not automatically be generalized to all scholarships. A deadline for one fee should not automatically be generalized to every fee.

Conditional language matters. Terms such as "may," "where permitted," "unless," "subject to approval," and "ordinarily" indicate that a provision may not operate as an unconditional guarantee.

RuleLens should prefer exact evidence over broad semantic similarity. A retrieved passage that discusses scholarships may still be insufficient to answer a question about renewal eligibility. A passage about attendance may not answer a question about placement eligibility.

When the corpus contains multiple sources, the system should preserve source identity, document name, section, and page information whenever available. If two sources conflict, both should be shown to the reader.

The regulations do not authorize RuleLens to make final administrative decisions. The system provides evidence-based navigation of the corpus and should direct unresolved matters to the appropriate University authority.

## 18. Records, Privacy, and Administrative Responsibility

Academic and administrative records should be maintained by the office responsible for the relevant process. Students should provide accurate information and should not knowingly submit false documents.

Access to records may be limited according to the University's administrative procedures. These general regulations do not establish a complete privacy policy or define every category of confidential information.

Students should protect their account credentials and official documents. If a student suspects unauthorized access, the student should use the University's designated security or administrative support channel.

A record correction request should identify the record in question and provide supporting evidence. The responsible office determines whether the correction is authorized.

Administrative responsibility is distributed across offices. Academic records belong primarily to the Academic Office; fees and financial account matters belong primarily to the Finance Office; scholarships belong primarily to the Financial Aid Office; examinations belong primarily to the Examination Office; and residential matters belong primarily to Student Affairs or the residential administration.

A student should not assume that one office can change a record controlled by another office. Cross-office processes may require coordination.

Where these regulations do not specify a response time, RuleLens must not invent one. The appropriate response is to state that the corpus does not specify the requested timeframe and identify the responsible authority where possible.

The University's official notices may contain dates, amounts, procedures, or exceptions not included in these general regulations. Such notices should be treated as separate sources and evaluated for scope and authority.

## 19. Student Support and Advising

Students may seek academic advising concerning course selection, progression, registration, and other academic matters. Advising is intended to help students understand requirements and make informed decisions.

An advisor's guidance should not be represented as a formal amendment to a University regulation unless the advisor has authority to issue the relevant decision.

Students who are uncertain about registration, progression, or graduation requirements should consult the official academic record and applicable program information. A general regulation may not contain every program-specific requirement.

The University may provide support services for academic difficulties, financial concerns, residential issues, and other student needs. This corpus does not establish eligibility criteria for every support program.

Students should keep copies of important communications and submissions. When a process requires a request, application, or appeal, evidence of submission may be useful for later review.

A student who receives conflicting information from different sources should identify the sources and ask the responsible authority to clarify which provision applies. RuleLens is designed to surface such conflicts rather than resolve them by confidence.

The existence of student support does not create a guarantee of a particular outcome. For example, academic advising does not guarantee permission to add a course, approval of a leave request, or eligibility for a scholarship.

## 20. Final Operational Principles

The regulations should be applied with attention to evidence, scope, and authority. Students should rely on exact provisions rather than assumptions based on common university practice.

A regulation is answerable only when the available evidence is sufficient to support the requested claim. A related passage is not necessarily sufficient evidence.

If the corpus is silent, the correct conclusion is that the corpus does not cover the question. RuleLens should not fill a gap with general knowledge, web search, or an invented University practice.

If two provisions conflict, RuleLens should identify both provisions and explain the nature of the conflict. It should not choose a winner unless the corpus itself provides a clear hierarchy, supersession clause, effective date, or authorized exception that resolves the apparent conflict.

If a provision is conditional, RuleLens should preserve the condition. "May" should not become "will." "Subject to approval" should not become "guaranteed." "Ordinarily" should not become "always."

If a source contains a table, the table is evidence. A deadline or fee appearing in a table should retain its row and column context during ingestion so that a retrieval system does not separate a date from the fee item to which it belongs.

PDF, Markdown, and other document formats are all part of the corpus. Source provenance should survive extraction so that users can inspect where an answer originated.

These final principles are especially important for RuleLens evaluation. The objective is not to maximize the number of questions answered. The objective is to maximize the number of questions answered correctly, refused appropriately, or identified as contradictory, with evidence that a reader can verify.



# Appendix A — Administrative Definitions

For purposes of these regulations, "Academic Office" means the office responsible for central academic administration. "Academic Council" means the University's academic policy-making body. "Academic year" means the period covered by the published annual academic calendar. "Applicable notice" means an official notice that expressly applies to the process or population in question.

"Approved" means that the responsible authority has granted permission through the applicable process. Submission of an application or request is not the same as approval.

"Calendar day" means a day counted consecutively on the calendar. "Working day" means a day on which the responsible University office ordinarily conducts official business, excluding days identified as University holidays or closures where the applicable procedure so provides. This corpus does not establish a complete holiday calendar.

"Course" means an academic unit in which a student is registered. "Program" means the degree or academic program in which the student is enrolled. "Regular examination" means the scheduled end-semester examination conducted under the ordinary academic calendar.

"Special examination" means an examination approved outside the ordinary examination schedule under the applicable special-examination procedure. Approval is not automatic.

"Backlog" means an unresolved academic requirement classified as such under Section 8.2. The term should not be expanded to include every low grade or incomplete administrative task unless the responsible academic record identifies it as a backlog.

"Fee deadline" means the deadline attached to the relevant fee item. A deadline for one fee must not be generalized to another fee without evidence.

"Scholarship notice" means an official notice establishing eligibility, deadlines, or other conditions for a particular scholarship cycle. Scholarship conditions may differ by scholarship.

"Registration hold" means an administrative restriction placed on a student's account under an applicable rule. A hold may affect registration or other processes, depending on the reason and responsible office.

# Appendix B — Evidence Handling Notes for RuleLens

The corpus intentionally contains both general regulations and narrower notices. The main regulation states one semester tuition deadline, while the separate fee notice states another. The main regulation states one merit scholarship GPA threshold, while the separate scholarship notice states another. The main regulation states a general 75% attendance threshold, while the separate attendance circular states 80%. These are deliberate test cases.

The presence of three contradictions does not mean that every difference between documents is a contradiction. A narrower rule can legitimately apply to a narrower population when its scope is explicit. For example, the merit scholarship requirement applies to the named scholarship and does not establish a universal scholarship rule. Likewise, a special examination can have additional conditions without contradicting the ordinary examination process if the conditions are explicitly limited to the special examination.

A retrieval system should preserve headings and nearby text. If it retrieves "20 September 2026" without the row label "Examination fee," the evidence is incomplete. If it retrieves "8.00/10.00" without the phrase "Demo University Merit Scholarship," the evidence may be insufficient for a question about that particular scholarship.

For source viewing, the system should retain document identifiers and page information for PDF-derived passages. Markdown sources may use section identifiers instead of physical pages. If page information is unavailable for a Markdown source, the system should say so rather than inventing a page number.

A user question can be semantically close to the corpus while still being unsupported. For example, the corpus discusses academic progression but does not establish a general minimum CGPA for placement registration. It discusses library borrowing but does not specify the maximum number of books. It discusses hostel conduct but does not specify the exact curfew time.

The desired behavior is conservative and verifiable. A concise refusal supported by an explanation of the missing provision is preferable to an elaborate answer based on assumptions.

# Appendix D — Responsible Use

RuleLens is an evidence-navigation prototype. It is not an authorized decision-maker and must not claim to issue University approvals, waive requirements, determine disciplinary outcomes, or provide legal advice.

A student facing a deadline should verify the applicable official notice and contact the responsible office when the corpus shows a conflict, ambiguity, or missing provision. The purpose of RuleLens is to make the evidence easier to inspect and to make uncertainty visible.

Where a question depends on a student's individual record, such as whether the student personally satisfies a progression requirement, the system should distinguish general rules from individualized facts. General regulations can explain the rule but cannot know facts that are not present in the corpus or user-provided record.

A trustworthy answer therefore has three components: a conclusion appropriate to the evidence, an explicit state indicating whether the corpus answers the question, and citations that let the reader inspect the supporting passage.
